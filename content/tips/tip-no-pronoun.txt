title: Spanish drops "I" and "you"

In Spanish, the verb's ending already tells you who is doing it.
Soy means I am and eres means you are, so you don't need a separate word for I or you.
To say I'm from Chile, just say soy de Chile.
Add yo (I) or tú (you) only when you want to stress the person or compare two people, as in English when you say I am, but you are.

example: Soy de México. | I'm from Mexico.
example: ¿Eres Ana? | Are you Ana?
example: Yo soy Luis y tú eres Ana. | I'm Luis, and you're Ana.
