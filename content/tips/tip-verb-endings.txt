title: Verbs change with who does them

A Spanish verb changes its ending to show who is doing it.
Quiero means I want, quieres means you want, and quiere means he or she wants.
Tener works the same way: tengo is I have, tienes is you have, and tiene is he or she has.
Most of the time, an ending in -o means I, an ending in -s means you, and an ending in -e means he or she.
Listen for the ending, because it tells you who, even when nobody says yo, tú or ella.

example: Quiero un café, por favor. | I want a coffee, please.
example: Tienes mi café. | You have my coffee.
example: Ella quiere un café y tiene mi café. | She wants a coffee and has my coffee.
