title: Talking about the past

To talk about something that already happened, Spanish changes the end of the verb.
For -ar verbs like hablar, drop the -ar and add -é for I or -ó for he or she, so hablé means I spoke and habló means he or she spoke.
Say the accent clearly, because hablo with no accent means I speak, right now.
Some very common verbs have their own short past forms, so learn them as words: tuve means I had, and hice means I did or I made.
Fue has two meanings: it was, and he or she went, and the rest of the sentence tells you which one.

example: Hablé con Ana y ella habló conmigo. | I talked to Ana and she talked to me.
example: Mi padre fue a la fiesta. | My father went to the party.
example: Hoy tuve mucho trabajo. | I had a lot of work today.
