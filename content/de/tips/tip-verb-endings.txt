title: Verbs change with who does them

In German, the verb changes a little depending on who is doing it.
The ending usually tells you who: -e for ich, -st for du and -t for er or sie, so ich habe, du hast, er hat.
Möchte is the odd one: ich möchte and er möchte are the same, and only du gets an ending, du möchtest.
When you speak, match the verb to the person, and when you listen, the ending helps you hear who it's about.

example: Ich habe ein Haus. | I have a house.
example: Sie hat Kaffee und ich habe Wasser. | She has coffee and I have water.
example: Du möchtest Kaffee, und er möchte Wasser. | You'd like coffee, and he'd like water.
