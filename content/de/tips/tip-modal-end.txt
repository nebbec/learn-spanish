title: Can, must, want: the second verb goes last

Words like können (can), müssen (must), wollen (want) and möchten (would like) often team up with a second verb.
The first verb changes with who does it, like ich kann or du kannst, and sits in its usual spot.
The second verb goes to the very end of the sentence in its dictionary form, so ich möchte Wasser trinken means I would like to drink water.
Everything else goes in between, and questions work the same way: Kannst du mir helfen? means can you help me?
English says to drink or to help, but German needs no word for to here.

example: Kannst du mir helfen? | Can you help me?
example: Ich möchte einen Kaffee trinken. | I'd like to drink a coffee.
example: Wir müssen Deutsch sprechen. | We have to speak German.
